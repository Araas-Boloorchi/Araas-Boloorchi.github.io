A quick understanding, not a deep dive. Automated Tire's platform is proprietary, so the specifics stay inside the company. What follows is a high-level summary of the kind of perception work I lead there, at the level already published on my resume. Happy to talk through my general approach in conversation.

I lead perception engineering for a robotic service platform that works on a vehicle without removing the wheel. That means owning the chain end to end: which sensors we use, the algorithms that read them, and getting both running reliably on the robot.

What the work involves

  • Sensor selection backed by measurement rather than spec sheets - I built a benchmarking platform that characterised seven depth cameras against the application's real requirements
  • That benchmarking drove a fleet-wide camera replacement, with roughly 50% better accuracy, a 20x higher frame rate, and far fewer connection failures
  • 3D reconstruction of a compressed tire's geometry to under 2 mm error - deceptively hard, because rubber under load does not hold a shape that deterministic measurement can pin down
  • An on-robot quality assessment that I took from a 30-minute runtime to real time
  • Object detection adapted to new camera perspectives, plus a region-of-interest step that improved both localisation and segmentation
  • A ROS 2 feedback pipeline with on-robot visualisation, and control logic that adjusts the robot's routine based on what perception reports back

Stack

  • Depth cameras, stereo vision, camera calibration
  • YOLOv8, PyTorch, OpenCV
  • ROS 2, Go, Python, C++
